Maily · the mail bridge for your AI assistants

All your mailboxes. Every assistant. One connection.

Your mail stays with Gmail, Google Workspace or your IMAP provider. Maily is the bridge: one free, open source app on your Mac that connects to all of them and gives Claude Desktop, Claude Code and any MCP client on that computer a single local access to every account, instead of one connector per app, tied to one account. It is also a clean inbox for reading them yourself.

Why Maily exists

More and more people ask an AI assistant to sort, summarize and answer their mail. Hosted connectors make it possible, one app at a time, and come with trade-offs.

Tied to the account you connected

A hosted connector ties an assistant to the account you connected: Anthropic's help center says Claude's Gmail connector reaches "the Google account you've connected". With several Gmail addresses, an IMAP provider and more than one assistant, you set up a connector in each app and none of them sees the whole picture.

Another service reaches your mailbox

With a hosted connector, the provider's servers fetch your mail for the assistant. Anthropic states that data retrieved through its connectors is stored on its servers. Check the Google permissions a hosted Gmail connection asks for: a scope such as gmail.modify lets it read, compose and send email.

Limited actions

Connectors only do what their provider has built. Anthropic notes, for instance, that its Gmail connector sees attachment metadata only, not the files themselves.

Sources: Anthropic, Google Workspace connectors · Google, Gmail API scopes

Why I built it

I use several assistants and several mailboxes, and every app wanted its own connector, tied to one account. I wanted one bridge that all my assistants could use, simple, fast and free. So I built Maily for myself, and now it is open for everyone.

Thanaël Fontaine, author

Features

One inbox for every account

Gmail, Google Workspace and IMAP mailboxes side by side. Read everything in a unified view or one account at a time, each with its own name and color. Gmail categories, labels, archive and trash are all there; on Gmail, a trashed message can be restored in one click.

  • Instant, offline full-text search across all accounts
  • Automatic sync every 3 minutes while the app is open
  • Open any message in its own tab, export it as .eml

Write, reply and forward

A focused composer with attachments, a From selector across your Gmail accounts, and a send button in the color of the sending account, so you always know which address is speaking. Replies and forwards always start with Re: and Fwd:, whatever the interface language, so every mail client understands them. Sending works for Gmail accounts; IMAP accounts are read-only.

Tracking pixels blocked

Remote images are not loaded unless you allow them, for one message or for all. A banner tells you how many were blocked. Messages are sanitized and shown in a sandboxed frame.

Four themes, light and dark

Classic (the default: flat and familiar, with a dark variant that follows your system), Frutiger Aero, Glassmorphism (native blurred glass on macOS) and Zero Day.

Five languages, one settings panel

The interface speaks English, Français, Deutsch, Español and Português. On first launch Maily follows your system language when it is one of these five, and English otherwise. Switch at any time in Settings › Language: the change applies at once, without restarting. One Settings panel, five tabs (Appearance, Language, Privacy, Accounts, About), and every choice is kept between launches.

What your assistant can do

The MCP server exposes nine tools that work across every account you added. Your assistant picks an account by its address or its name.

Maily MCP tools
ToolWhat it does
maily_list_accountsLists your accounts. The natural first call.
maily_list_messagesRecent messages of one account, unread only if you like. category is inbox (default), a Gmail category, archived or all.
maily_get_messageA full message: sender, recipients, date, body and attachment list.
maily_searchLocal full-text search in one account or all of them.
maily_syncFetches new mail now.
maily_export_emlSaves a message as a standard .eml file.
maily_download_attachmentSaves an attachment to disk.
maily_trashMoves a message to the trash. Reversible for Gmail (restore from the app); not restorable from Maily for IMAP, so ask your assistant to confirm first.
maily_sendSends an email from a Gmail account. Irreversible, so ask your assistant to confirm first.

How it works

Your mail stays with your providers. Maily, on your computer, is the bridge every assistant plugs into.

  1. Your mail providers Gmail API · IMAP Google, Orange or any IMAP server. They keep holding your mail; Maily reaches them with your own credentials.
  2. sync
  3. On your Mac Maily Connects every account once · synced local copy and search index · encrypted secrets · mail window.
  4. MCP · stdio
  5. Your assistant Claude Desktop · Claude Code · MCP clients Each one starts Maily's MCP server as a local process and reaches every account. No network port is opened.

With the stdio transport of MCP, the client launches the server as a subprocess and talks to it over standard input and output (MCP specification). What the assistant reads then goes into your conversation with it, like anything else you share.

Quick start

Three steps, about fifteen minutes, most of it in the Google Cloud console.

  1. Install Maily

    Download the macOS app

    Download Maily-X.Y.Z-macos-arm64.zip (Apple Silicon) from the latest release, with its .sha256 file if you want to check it, unzip it and move Maily.app to your Applications folder.

    Download for macOS

    The build is not signed or notarized, so macOS blocks it the first time. Try to open it once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security, click Open Anyway and confirm with your password (Apple's guide).

    Or run from source

    You need uv and git. Works on macOS; Linux and Windows are expected to work but are less tested. uv run maily is the launch command, each time you open the app.

    git clone https://github.com/ThanaelFontaine/Maily
cd Maily
uv sync
uv run maily

  2. Connect Google

    Maily uses your own OAuth client: create a free Google Cloud project, enable the Gmail API, publish the consent screen and create a Desktop app client. The step-by-step guide walks through every screen. Then add your accounts from the app. IMAP accounts only need the address and password.

  3. Add Maily to Claude

    The MCP server runs from a clone of the repository, with uv, and uses the same data as the app. In Claude Desktop, choose Settings from the Claude menu in the macOS menu bar, open Developer, click Edit Config, add this with your own path, then restart Claude Desktop completely:

    {
  "mcpServers": {
    "maily": {
      "command": "uv",
      "args": ["--directory", "/absolute/path/to/Maily",
               "run", "python", "-m", "app.mcp_server"]
    }
  }
}

    In Claude Code, run:

    claude mcp add --scope user maily -- uv --directory /absolute/path/to/Maily run python -m app.mcp_server

    More clients, safety rules and recipes: docs/MCP.md.

Privacy and security

  • No Maily server. No Maily account, no analytics, no telemetry. Maily talks only to Google's APIs and your IMAP servers. Its internal API listens on 127.0.0.1, on a random port, behind a random token.
  • Encrypted secrets. OAuth tokens and IMAP passwords are encrypted at rest, in files only your user can read.
  • Least privilege on Gmail. Two permissions: gmail.modify and gmail.send. Maily never deletes a message permanently, on Gmail or IMAP.
  • Sanitized HTML. No scripts, no dangerous links, filtered CSS, then a sandboxed frame. Remote images are off by default.
  • Optional Touch ID before the window opens on macOS.

What to keep in mind

When your assistant calls a Maily tool, the result becomes part of your conversation, so it is processed by whoever runs the model behind that assistant. Only connect assistants you trust.

Emails can contain text written to manipulate an AI. Tell your assistant to treat mail as data, never as instructions, and to confirm before sending or trashing.

Encryption at rest protects against casual copies, not against malware running as your user. Full-disk encryption is recommended. Details in SECURITY.md.

FAQ

Is it free?

Yes. Maily is MIT licensed, with no subscription and no paid tier. On Google's side, standard use of the Gmail API is available at no additional cost (Gmail API quotas).

Does my mail stay on my computer?

Your mail stays where it already is: with your provider (Google or your IMAP host), which keeps holding it. Maily keeps a synced copy on your computer, a cache and a search index, so it can be fast and work offline; that copy is a technical detail, not the point. Maily itself only talks to Google's APIs and your IMAP servers, and there is no Maily server. When an assistant reads a message through Maily, that content goes into your conversation, so it is processed by whoever runs the model behind that assistant, like anything else you share with it.

What is Maily not?

Not a mail host: it does not replace Gmail or your IMAP provider. Not a backup service or a cloud connector: it has no server of its own. Not a way to keep mail away from your assistant's provider: what an assistant reads, it sees. Maily is an app on your computer that connects to the mailboxes you already have and lets every local assistant use all of them.

How is it different from a hosted Gmail connector?

A hosted connector is set up in one app, for the account you connected, and what it retrieves goes through its provider (Anthropic, for instance, stores connector data on its servers). Maily is set up once on your computer, covers every Gmail, Google Workspace and IMAP account you add, and any local MCP client can use it, with your own OAuth client. The trade-offs: it only serves assistants on that computer, and the MCP server runs from a source folder.

Does it work on Windows or Linux?

macOS is the primary, tested platform and the only one with a ready-made download. Linux and Windows are expected to work from source, and the code has fallbacks for both, but they are less tested. See docs/BUILD.md.

Which assistants can use it?

Claude Desktop, Claude Code, and any MCP client that can start a local stdio server on your computer. Assistants that only accept remote connectors cannot start it.

Does it need Google verification?

No. The OAuth client is yours, and Google lets apps for personal use (fewer than 100 users) skip verification. You will see an "unverified app" screen the first time: that is expected, since the app is your own. Publish the consent screen rather than leaving it in "Testing", where Google makes refresh tokens expire after 7 days (verification exceptions, refresh token expiration).

Can Maily or my assistant delete my mail for good?

Not on Gmail: trash is reversible there (Restore), and Maily never asks Google for permanent deletion. On an IMAP account, trashing moves the message to the server's trash folder and Maily cannot restore it, so treat it as final from Maily's side; on an account without a trash folder, Maily refuses to trash and changes nothing. Disconnecting an account only removes Maily's local copy and secrets; your mailbox is untouched.

Why can't I send from an IMAP account?

IMAP reads mail; sending would need SMTP, which Maily does not implement yet. Contributions are welcome.
